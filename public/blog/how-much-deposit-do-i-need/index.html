How Much Deposit Do You Really Need?

An in-depth analysis of 5%, 10%, and 20% deposit scenarios, government schemes, stamp duty concessions, and practical deposit planning checklists for Australian buyers.

Demystifying Australia's Property Deposit Rules

One of the most persistent myths in Australian real estate is that you must save a 20% cash deposit before applying for a home loan. While saving 20% eliminates Lenders Mortgage Insurance (LMI) and provides access to lower baseline interest rates, waiting years to accumulate $150,000+ while property prices appreciate can actually move home ownership further out of reach.

Thanks to government initiatives like the First Home Guarantee, family security guarantees, and specialized low-deposit credit policies, eligible Australian first home buyers can enter the market with as little as 5% cash upfront. Understanding how different deposit tiers affect your monthly mortgage, LMI costs, and total interest paid empowers you to choose the entry pathway that best balances cash speed against long-term borrowing cost.

1. Comparing 5%, 10%, and 20% Deposit Scenarios

To evaluate which deposit percentage aligns with your goals, let's examine the financial mechanics of buying a $700,000 home across three standard deposit scenarios:

Scenario A: The 20% Deposit ($140,000 Cash Equity)

  • Loan Amount: $560,000 (80% LVR).
  • LMI Payable: $0.
  • Pros: Zero mortgage insurance cost, lowest tier interest rates, lower monthly principal & interest repayments, strong immediate equity buffer.
  • Cons: Can take 5 to 8 years of disciplined saving to accumulate $140k + transaction fees, risking property market price inflation.

Scenario B: The 10% Deposit ($70,000 Cash Equity)

  • Loan Amount: $630,000 (90% LVR).
  • LMI Payable: ~$14,000 (if capitalized onto loan, total loan becomes $644,000).
  • Pros: Reaches property market 2 to 4 years faster than a 20% deposit; modest equity margin protects against minor market dips.
  • Cons: LMI premium incurred (unless backed by a government guarantee or professional waiver); slightly higher monthly repayments.

Scenario C: The 5% Deposit ($35,000 Cash Equity)

  • Loan Amount: $665,000 (95% LVR).
  • LMI Payable: $0 under First Home Guarantee, or ~$28,000 if using commercial 95% LMI underwriting.
  • Pros: Fastest entry into home ownership; requires minimal upfront cash.
  • Cons: Maximum borrowing tier requires flawless credit history and strong income serviceability. Commercial 95% LMI is expensive if a scheme place is unavailable.

2. Government Assistance & First Home Buyer Schemes

Federal and state governments provide significant financial incentives to reduce deposit barriers for Australian citizens and permanent residents.

The Home Guarantee Scheme (HGS)

Administered by Housing Australia, the Home Guarantee Scheme enables eligible first home buyers to purchase a home with a 5% deposit without paying LMI. Key components include:

  • First Home Guarantee (FHBG): 35,000 places per financial year for eligible single applicants (earning up to $125k) or joint applicants (earning up to $200k combined). Regional and capital city price caps apply (e.g., $900,000 cap in Sydney/capital NSW).
  • Regional First Home Buyer Guarantee (RFHBG): Dedicated allocation for buyers purchasing in regional regional areas outside major capital cities.
  • Family Home Guarantee (FHG): Enables eligible single parents or legal guardians with dependents to purchase with as little as a 2% deposit without LMI.

Stamp Duty Exemptions & Concessions

Stamp duty (transfer duty) is the single largest transaction expense for Australian property buyers. Most states offer generous concessions for first home buyers:

  • NSW (First Home Buyers Assistance Scheme): Full stamp duty exemption on new and existing homes priced up to $800,000, with concessional sliding scale rates up to $1,000,000.
  • VIC / QLD / WA: Similar tiered thresholds providing full or partial duty exemptions for entry-level properties. Use our Stamp Duty Calculator to check exact state savings.

3. Step-by-Step Deposit Planning Checklist

  1. Calculate Your Purchase Target: Establish your maximum price limit based on borrowing power assessments.
  2. Determine Total Cash Required: Add your target deposit (5%, 10%, or 20%) to estimated conveyancing ($2,000), building inspections ($800), and non-exempt stamp duty fees.
  3. Check Scheme Eligibility: Verify whether your taxable income, citizenship status, and property purchase price meet federal First Home Guarantee and state grant criteria.
  4. Verify Genuine Savings: Ensure at least 5% of the purchase price has been held in your savings account or FHSS super account for 3+ months.
  5. Reserve an Emergency Buffer: Keep $10,000 to $20,000 untouched in reserve for unexpected post-settlement costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I buy a home with a 5% deposit?

Yes. You can buy with a 5% deposit by securing a spot under the First Home Guarantee scheme (0% LMI) or by utilizing commercial 95% LVR lending with LMI capitalized onto the loan.

Are gifted deposits from parents accepted by banks?

Yes, gifted deposits are accepted by Australian lenders. However, if borrowing over 85% LVR, banks typically require you to hold at least 5% in genuine savings or show a strong 12-month rental history.

What property price caps apply under the First Home Guarantee in NSW?

In Sydney and major NSW regional centres (Illawarra, Newcastle), the purchase price cap for the First Home Guarantee is $900,000, while the rest of state cap is $750,000.
