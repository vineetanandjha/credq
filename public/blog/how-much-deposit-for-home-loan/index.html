Deposit planning

How much deposit do you need for a home loan?

The right deposit target depends on the property price, your loan structure and the cash you need to keep aside for the purchase.

The 20% deposit benchmark

A 20% deposit means borrowing 80% of the property value, commonly described as an 80% loan-to-value ratio (LVR). This may avoid lenders mortgage insurance and can give you more lender options. It is a benchmark, not a rule: many lenders consider loans above 80% LVR.

Low-deposit home loans

Depending on eligibility and lender policy, a buyer may purchase with a smaller deposit. The trade-off can include lenders mortgage insurance, a higher interest rate or stricter evidence of genuine savings. Eligible buyers may also explore current government support schemes, noting that rules, places and property price caps can change.

Do not spend every dollar on the deposit

Keep a separate allowance for stamp duty where payable, transfer and registration fees, conveyancing, inspections, loan establishment costs and moving. A modest cash buffer after settlement can also make the first months of ownership less stressful.

Calculate the numbers for your target property

For a $800,000 purchase, a 20% deposit is $160,000 and the loan is $640,000 before fees. With a 10% deposit, the loan is $720,000 and additional costs such as lenders mortgage insurance may apply. The property valuation used by the lender can also affect the final LVR.
