First Home Buyer Hub — Step 5

The Real Costs of Buying Your First Home

A complete breakdown of government taxes, legal representation fees, pre-purchase inspections, bank settlement charges, moving costs, and ongoing property ownership expenses in Australia.

Planning Beyond the Purchase Contract Price

When buying a property in Australia, the agreed contract purchase price is only one component of your overall financial commitment. First home buyers frequently calculate their savings based strictly on the required loan deposit, only to be surprised by thousands of dollars in unavoidable transaction expenses prior to settlement day.

At CREdQ, our mortgage specialists ensure first home buyers receive a detailed Upfront Funds Position Statement before making unconditional property offers. Factoring in stamp duty, conveyancing fees, inspection charges, loan establishment costs, and initial utility connections prevents last-minute cash shortfalls at settlement.

1. Government Taxes & Transfer Duty (Stamp Duty)

Transfer duty (commonly known as stamp duty) is a state government tax levied on property acquisitions. Stamp duty varies based on state legislation, property price, and whether you purchase a vacant block, new home, or established house.

First Home Buyer Concessions

Most state governments offer valuable exemptions or sliding-scale concessions for first home buyers:

  • NSW (First Home Buyers Assistance Scheme): Full exemption on properties priced up to $800,000, saving up to $30,735 in tax. Partial concessions apply between $800,000 and $1,000,000.
  • State Transfer & Registration Fees: In addition to duty, state land registries charge nominal land title transfer and mortgage registration fees (typically $300 to $400 combined).

Use our Stamp Duty Calculator to check your state's exact stamp duty concession thresholds.

2. Legal & Conveyancing Representation

Engaging a qualified property solicitor or licensed conveyancer is essential to manage legal title transfers, review contract terms, perform property title searches, and execute settlement via PEXA.

Conveyancing Fee Breakdown

Expense Item Typical Cost Range Description
Professional Legal Fee $1,000 – $1,800 Contract analysis, title transfer, legal advice, settlement coordination.
Out-of-Pocket Disbursements $400 – $700 Title searches, council rate certificates, water authority searches, PEXA platform fees.
Total Legal & Conveyancing $1,400 – $2,500 Required prior to settlement.

3. Pre-Purchase Inspection Reports

Never skip independent property inspections. Identifying structural defects, termite infestations, or unapproved building works prior to exchanging contracts saves tens of thousands in future repairs.

  • Building & Pest Inspection: $500 to $900 for a licensed inspector to assess structural integrity, roof timber, dampness, and timber pest activity.
  • Strata Inspection Report: $250 to $400 for apartments or townhouses to audit strata financial registers, capital works funds, special levies, and building defect claims.

4. Lender Establishment & Mortgage Settlement Fees

While many Australian lenders offer package deals with waived upfront fees, standard mortgage establishment costs may include:

  • Loan Application / Establishment Fee: $0 to $600.
  • Property Valuation Fee: $0 to $300 (often waived for standard suburban dwellings).
  • Lender Legal & Settlement Fee: $150 to $350 for bank settlement execution.

5. Moving & Ongoing Property Ownership Costs

Once settlement completes, ongoing ownership expenses replace your previous rent payments:

Ongoing Ownership Budgeting

  • Council Rates: $1,500 – $2,500 per year for local municipal services.
  • Water Supply Charges: $700 – $1,200 per year plus usage consumption.
  • Building & Contents Insurance: $1,200 – $2,500 per year (building insurance is mandatory prior to mortgage loan drawdown).
  • Strata Levies (Apartments/Townhouses): $800 – $2,500 per quarter covering common property maintenance, building insurance, and sinking funds.
  • Moving Expenses: $800 – $2,000 for professional removalists or vehicle rental.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I budget for legal and conveyancing fees?

We recommend budgeting $1,500 to $2,500 for professional legal representation and statutory disbursements during a residential property purchase in Australia.

Are building and pest inspections compulsory?

Building and pest inspections are not legally mandatory, but they are essential to protect your investment by identifying costly structural flaws or pest damage before contracts become binding.

When must home building insurance be activated?

Lenders require proof of active building insurance policies naming the bank as an interested financial party prior to releasing settlement funds on a standalone house.
