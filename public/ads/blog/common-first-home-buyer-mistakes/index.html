Common First Home Buyer Mistakes to Avoid

Protect your deposit and mortgage approval by learning how to avoid the 10 most expensive errors first-time property buyers make in Australia.

Navigating the Property Market Without Expensive Pitfalls

Buying your first property is an exciting milestone, but navigating real estate contracts, bank credit rules, and auction conditions can be fraught with hidden traps. Even small misunderstandings regarding how lenders calculate serviceability or inspect contract terms can result in delayed settlements, lost deposits, or loan rejections.

The 10 Most Critical Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Bidding at Auction Without Pre-Approval: Auction bids are legally unconditional. If you win at auction without formal bank pre-approval, your deposit is at risk if loan approval falls through. (See Pre-Approval Guide).
  2. Applying for New Credit Before Settlement: Taking out a new car loan, personal loan, or BNPL service between pre-approval and settlement alters your financial profile and can trigger loan cancellation.
  3. Underestimating Upfront Purchasing Costs: Forgetting to budget for conveyancing ($2,000), building inspections ($800), and non-exempt stamp duty leads to last-minute cash panic. Review our Real Costs of Buying Guide.
  4. Maintaining High Credit Card Limits: Keeping an unused $15,000 credit card limit reduces your borrowing power by ~$70,000 regardless of your zero balance.
  5. Skipping Pre-Purchase Building & Pest Inspections: Failing to inspect roof structures, dampness, or termite damage can leave you with tens of thousands in urgent repairs post-settlement.
  6. Assuming Online Calculators Provide Guaranteed Rates: Generic online borrowing estimators do not apply APRA 3.0% serviceability buffers or lender-specific income shading rules. Use an accredited broker or our Borrowing Power Calculator.
  7. Changing Employment During the Application Phase: Switching employers or moving from PAYG to contracting right before lodging a mortgage application resets serviceability tenure requirements with major banks.
  8. Not Checking Government Scheme Price Caps: Bidding $5,000 over a state stamp duty waiver cap or First Home Guarantee limit forfeits thousands of dollars in government benefits.
  9. Draining All Liquid Emergency Cash: Using 100% of your savings for a deposit leaves zero buffer for post-settlement council rates, moving fees, or urgent maintenance.
  10. Approaching Only One Bank: Relying solely on your primary transaction bank means missing out on superior borrowing limits, LMI waivers, or lower interest rates available across 50+ alternative lenders.
