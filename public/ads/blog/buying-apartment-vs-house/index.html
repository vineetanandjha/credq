Buying an Apartment vs House

Weigh up the entry price, strata levies, maintenance responsibilities, capital growth trends, and bank lending policies when choosing between a house and an apartment.

Apartment vs House: Which Property Fits Your Strategy?

When entering the Australian property market, deciding whether to purchase an apartment (unit or townhouse) or a freestanding house is a key decision. Your choice impacts your required cash deposit, ongoing quarterly expenses, long-term capital growth, and bank valuation policies.

1. Buying an Apartment (Unit / Townhouse)

  • Lower Entry Purchase Price: Apartments in major metropolitan areas like Sydney or Melbourne provide a significantly lower price entry point, allowing buyers to enter desirable inner-city suburbs sooner.
  • Strata Levies: Apartment owners must pay ongoing quarterly strata levies ($800 to $2,500/quarter) to fund common property maintenance, building insurance, and capital works reserves.
  • Lender Restrictions: Banks enforce strict minimum internal floor size policies (e.g., minimum 40–50sqm internal area excluding balcony/parking) and high-density postcode concentration limits.

2. Buying a House

  • Land Value & Capital Growth: Houses own a higher underlying land content, which historically drives stronger long-term capital appreciation compared to high-density units.
  • No Strata Levies: You maintain full control over property modifications and pay no strata fees, although building maintenance and home insurance fall entirely on your shoulders.
  • Higher Purchase Price: Demands a larger deposit and higher borrowing capacity.
