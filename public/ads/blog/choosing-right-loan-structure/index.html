Why Loan Structure Matters As Much As Interest Rates

When shopping for a mortgage, many buyers focus exclusively on finding the lowest advertised interest rate. However, your home loan structure—including repayment type, offset account setup, redraw flexibility, and split options—determines how much interest you actually pay over 30 years and how easily you can adapt if your life circumstances change.

1. Principal and Interest (P&I) vs Interest-Only (IO)

  • Principal and Interest: Standard repayment type for owner-occupiers. Every monthly payment pays down both the underlying loan debt and the interest charged, building home equity continuously.
  • Interest-Only: Typically restricted to property investors or short-term bridging scenarios. Monthly payments cover only interest charges, leaving the principal balance unchanged.

2. Offset Accounts and Redraw Facilities

Combining a variable rate loan with a 100% offset account allows your daily cash transaction balance to directly reduce the loan principal subject to interest. For a full comparison, read Offset Account vs Redraw: What is the Difference?.
