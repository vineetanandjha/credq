Choosing the Right Rate Structure for Your First Mortgage

When obtaining your first Australian home loan, deciding between a fixed interest rate and a variable interest rate is a major financial decision. Both options come with distinct advantages, restriction trade-offs, and risk profiles.

1. Fixed Rate Home Loans

A fixed rate mortgage locks in your interest rate and exact monthly repayment for a set period (typically 1 to 5 years). Regardless of whether the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) raises or lowers cash rates, your repayments remain unchanged.

  • Pros: Guaranteed repayment certainty, immunity against interest rate hikes, simplified household budgeting.
  • Cons: Limited extra repayment caps (often $10,000/year max), restricted offset account access, potential economic break costs if you refinance or sell during the fixed term.

2. Variable Rate Home Loans

With a variable rate loan, your interest rate fluctuates in response to RBA rate decisions and individual bank funding costs.

  • Pros: Unlimited extra repayments, 100% full offset account compatibility, redraw facility access, no break costs if you sell or refinance.
  • Cons: Repayments will increase if the RBA or your lender raises interest rates.

3. The Best of Both Worlds: Split Home Loans

Many first home buyers opt for a split loan structure (e.g., 60% fixed and 40% variable). This provides repayment certainty on the fixed portion while preserving full offset account savings benefits and extra repayment flexibility on the variable portion.
